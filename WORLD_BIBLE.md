# World bible — The Last Forecast revision

This replaces the earlier Ninth Relay rescue outline. Retained original story consists of the Mossglass chapter, its optional courier testimony and inspectable notes; the current sky layout uses the user-requested Hyrule reference setting. Everything explicitly marked planned below is future content.

## World and visual rules

The old network did not create stable weather without cost: it transferred dangerous pressure, water and heat between catchments. Its census decided which places could absorb those costs. Nineteen years after the fracture, people and ecosystems occupy its supposedly empty spaces. Restarting old commands without changing that authority would repeat the disaster.

Surface Marches: lived-in routes, repair yards, mineral pools and flood terraces. Drifting Crown: exposed suspended reservoirs, broken observatories and communities dependent on the new wind. Rootsea: inhabited maintenance routes and living roots around the buried network. Only the compact surface basin is currently playable; floating silhouettes are scenery, and the hollow is an outdoor root grove rather than an underground biome.

Ancient architecture uses ceramic structural ribs, copper joints, service-height markings and mineral conduits. New construction uses woven textiles, reused brackets and deliberately mismatched repairs. Weathered surfaces should show use and load. Interactive shapes and text supplement color. Music and sound are original synthesis; no extracted Nintendo assets.

## Implemented cast

- Iri Vale: expedition mechanic and Eda’s trusted partner. Their action is to investigate, improvise a route and put a local decision into working machinery. Appearance/pronoun customization is not implemented.
- Eda Rook: a researcher who filed an objection and deliberately disconnected Bellwether’s remote command. She continued toward the Census Engine. Her courier explains why; her exact present location remains unknown. She has acted before Iri arrives, rather than waiting to be rescued.
- Sella Venn: researcher sheltering people excluded from the network’s records. She wants both reliable supplies and a living hollow, and admits the present regulator cannot deliver both. Her dry humor concerns practical problems. She receives and keeps the local regulator.

## Implemented chapter: The Last Forecast

Eda’s returned courier contains a field objection and a two-panel crossing pattern. A moving ceramic weight operates its archive plate; Echo can return its recorded motion, while Threadbind offers an alternate solution. Bellwether’s ledger describes a scheduled balancing command. Eda’s testimony changes its interpretation from natural prediction to imposed allocation.

The player chooses a root spillway or a surface crossing. The former sustains an updraft; the latter reopens a physical road. Sella acknowledges the corresponding benefit and cost. The chapter ends with local control and reinforced travel equipment. Existing route/reward save facts retain their meanings and remain completed after migration.

## Planned campaign: authority without a throne

Act II, Many Homes: investigate four distinct communities, in flexible order, whose repairs conflict. A Crown courier needs predictable winds; a furnace settlement needs cooling water; a wetland cooperative protects a nursery; a desert transport community depends on underground refuges. Each must have its own playable crisis, ally, spatial dungeon and lasting route change before it is counted as content. Learn different forms of local decision-making rather than collecting four interchangeable keys.

Act III, The Uncounted: descend through Rootsea maintenance settlements that historically paid the network’s costs. Recover the physical evidence of those transfers. Eda tried correcting the census; the central engine rejected living testimony that did not match its categories. She is now holding a maintenance interruption open, with help from people below. Her solution is incomplete, not secretly infallible.

The antagonist is provisionally the Underwriter: a surviving supervisory intelligence charged with keeping the greatest documented population safe. Its records are biased, its authority unaccountable, and its methods increasingly coercive. It preserves some communities while sacrificing those it cannot classify. Its threat must be demonstrated through understandable infrastructure and encounters, not exposition alone. This character has no implemented model, dialogue encounter or boss.

Act IV, A Working Horizon: ally contributions let the player isolate and reconnect local anchors. The finale combines taught movement, construction, return of machinery and combat. Rescue Eda through an interactive maintenance route; do not invent a new indispensable last-minute power. The central choice is to distribute decision-making and accept negotiated, imperfect repairs. Regional epilogues reflect actual saved outcomes. No ending cinematic, postgame state or regional campaign beyond Mossglass is currently implemented.

## Content discipline

Forecasts do not imply an implemented countdown. Ghostly recordings do not imply time travel. Echo returns object transforms, not history. New locations need silhouettes, routes, mechanical purpose, rewards and recovery. Any future rewrite must preserve or explicitly migrate persistent facts; it must not erase a completed expedition to force new exposition.
